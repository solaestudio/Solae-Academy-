01 · What's inside

What's in this bank

These aren't made-up practice prompts. They're real, high-performing UGC videos that brands actually paid for and ran.

Canvas Examples

Talking Heads, Text-On-Screens, Green Screens, Talking Head Demos and Silent Demos.

Traditional UGC Examples

28 videos sorted by niche (beauty, body care, fragrance, fitness, beverages and loungewear) in styles like talking head, voiceover, trending audio and lifestyle.

Why this gives you an edge

  • Your portfolio looks like real industry work. Most new creators fill their portfolios with random videos that don't look like anything brands actually run. Yours will look like you're an experienced creator.
  • Hiring leads will recognize the style. The UGC world is small. Many brand and agency leads will know these campaigns and formats, so they might assume you've worked with brands before.
  • You'll stand out from other beginners. While other new creators are still figuring out what a good UGC video looks like, you'll show up with a portfolio built on proven, high-engagement content.
One rule

If a brand asks if you were on those campaigns, be upfront. These are your own recreations of popular campaign formats (spec work), not gigs you were hired for. But it still shows you understand exactly what converts, and that's what gets you hired.

03 · Filming guide

Before you film

Two rules matter most: good lighting, and replicating the examples as closely as possible.

Filming

  • Face a window or a ring light so light falls on the front of your face.
  • Avoid having a bright window or lamp behind you so you're not backlit.
  • Check there are no harsh shadows on your face or the product.
  • Film a 5-second test clip and watch it back before recording the full take.
  • Talk normally like you're on FaceTime with a friend. Don't speak softly. Be clear and articulate.
  • Cut out the dead space, and follow the editing style of the example like captions, title, etc.

Replicate the examples

  • Match the framing: how close the camera is and where you sit in the shot.
  • Match the pacing: how fast the cuts and talking are.
  • Match the style: text placement, product angles and on-screen actions.
  • When unsure, re-watch the example and copy it rather than improvising.

05 · Traditional UGC examples

Traditional UGC

Pick 4 videos from this section: 4 different products and ideally multiple video styles.

These examples use everyday products you likely already own. The videos are organized by niche below, so start with the niche that fits you best (beauty, fitness, wellness and so on).

  1. 01.

    Choose 4 products

    Choose 4 different products you already have at home, ideally ones that fit your niche interests.

  2. 02.

    Mix your styles

    Try to do multiple video styles. For example one or two talking head, one voiceover, one trending audio, etc. Switching it up shows brands your range as a creator.

  3. 03.

    Pick your videos

    Pick the videos you want to recreate from the examples below and choose based off the niche you want to position yourself in. Keep in mind the product you have at home will likely not be the exact product shown: if the example shows a perfume, use any perfume you own. The same goes for water bottles, workout sets, lip liners and every other category.

How to film them

  • Copy everything else exactly. Match the script, pacing, editing, cuts and camera angles as closely as you can. These are proven videos with high engagement.
  • Swap only the product details. Whenever the script mentions the brand name, product name or features, change them to accurately describe the product you are showing.
  • Two products in one video? If the example showcases multiple products in video, make sure all the products you show are from the same brand so it looks like a real sponsored video.
  • Do this for all 4 videos.
